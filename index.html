Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What are gauged porcelain tile panels?+

Gauged porcelain tile panels (GPTP) are large format, ultra-thin porcelain surfaces typically 3–6mm thick. They come in massive sizes — up to 5×10 feet — and replicate the look of marble, stone, concrete, and other materials with superior durability and zero maintenance.

Why can't any tile contractor install these?+

Gauged porcelain panels require specialized training, tools, and techniques that differ significantly from standard tile. The panels are thin, extremely large, and expensive — a single mishandled panel can be a costly mistake. Proper installation requires manufacturer-specific adhesives, substrate preparation, and handling methods most contractors have never been trained on.

What certifications do you hold?+

Rich Smith holds manufacturer certifications from Laminam, Atlas Concorde, StonePeak, Dekton, Schluter Systems, Laticrete, and Sicis Glass Panels. He is an NTCA member with multiple certifications, and has completed advanced training at both the Crossville plant in Tennessee and Tile Nation in Nebraska.

How do porcelain panels compare to natural marble?+

Porcelain panels replicate the look of natural marble with remarkable accuracy while offering significant advantages: non-porous (no sealing required), stain resistant, scratch resistant, UV resistant, heat resistant, and lighter weight. Natural marble requires regular sealing and can stain and etch.

Do porcelain panels require maintenance?+

Virtually none. Porcelain panels are non-porous and never need sealing. They resist stains, mold, mildew, and bacteria — making them especially well-suited for showers and South Florida's humid climate.

How long does installation take?+

Timeline varies by scope. A feature wall or fireplace surround might take 1–2 days. A full shower including waterproofing typically takes 3–5 days. We provide a clear timeline during the estimate process.

What areas do you serve?+

We serve all of South Florida including Miami-Dade, Broward, and Palm Beach counties. Primary areas include Fort Lauderdale, Weston, Parkland, Boca Raton, Palm Beach, Miami, Miami Beach, Coral Gables, and waterfront communities along the Intracoastal.
